# Conclusions and recommendation

**Recommendation.** Do not change the latent-prior architecture on this stress test alone: the assignment's formal outcome remains inconclusive. Retain the existing small AdaLN MLP as the baseline, define a better discriminating success criterion prospectively, and investigate regularized PCA whitening next. Its lower measured errors make it a promising follow-up, not a demonstrated requirement for a structured prior. The all-fail branch requires withholding a method verdict; this study does not establish that TimeFlow needs S4, or that either pretrained system fails.

In the five-seed primary GP-source comparison, MLP residual MSE is 0.04927 versus 0.05827 for S4. Whitening reduces MLP residual MSE by **18.6%** and PC1 MSE by **8.8%**, while generated roughness remains close to the target. The paired residual-error difference for whitening is −0.00918, with a seed-bootstrap 95% interval of [−0.00961, −0.00855]. Roughness differences do not establish a separate whitening advantage. These intervals describe optimization variability on one fixed data draw; they are not uncertainty across GP families or real datasets.

The lower-learning-rate check matters: nine warm restarts improve validation objectives, so 6,000 updates cannot be claimed to establish convergence. Across the three restarted seeds, whitened MLP still has the lowest mean PC1 and residual errors, and S4 still has higher mean residual error than the ordinary MLP. The original checkpoints and five-seed results remain unchanged. Better coordinate weighting is a useful intervention here, but this does not prove a particular Jacobian or unused-dimension mechanism.

The shorter-lengthscale follow-up passes the geometry gate (GP velocity E₂=0.946), but again yields 0/15 literal successes and 15/15 roughness passes. GP-source residual MSE is 0.10859 for whitened MLP, 0.12372 for MLP and 0.13110 for S4. S4 now has slightly lower PC1 error than MLP, so head ordering depends on the metric. Shortening the lengthscale does not resolve the success-rule problem.

The literal two-sided raw 20% rule passes **0/25** primary runs, although **25/25** meet the roughness condition. Residual error is much smaller than PC1 error, rather than too large. The privileged analytic GP reference also fails that equality test. A secondary one-sided “residual no worse” reading would pass all 25, illustrating that raw error equality is not a reliable discriminator in this anisotropic geometry. Neither interpretation is substituted retrospectively for the declared primary flag.

Roughness agreement alone is insufficient: oracle Euler integration can suppress conditional variance while retaining near-correct adjacent jumps. The oracle also sees raw-history information unavailable to the networks. Normalized diagnostics and whitening depend on the declared eigenvalue floor; ten of sixteen GP directions are floored. We therefore withhold a general architectural or distributional-accuracy verdict. The optional real-series extension is omitted; further work should validate conditional calibration, additional data draws and tuning budgets before extending this recommendation.
